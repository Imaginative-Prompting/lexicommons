Privacy policy

Last updated November 12, 2026

  • No ads, no tracking, no analytics. We don’t sell or rent anything about you, and nothing on the site loads from another company to watch you.
  • An email address only if you add one. You sign in with a passkey, Google or GitHub. An email you add is only for getting back into your account and for receipts, and no one else ever sees it.
  • Your progress, your packs and your texts are kept with your account, so they’re there on every device you sign in on: your answers, your streak, your place on the path, your word bank, where you are in each book, the packs you or your AI make, the decks you add, and the texts you add. With the plan’s Lexicommons Cloud, the rest of what your devices sync is kept on our servers too.
  • We keep what your account needs, and what you ask us to keep for you, or an AI you connect.
  • What you share with the community is public, under CC BY-SA 4.0.
  • If you buy the plan, Stripe takes the payment, with an email for its receipts. We never see your card.
  • Download what we hold, or delete your account, any time, in Settings → Your data.

Who we are

Lexicommons (lexicommons.com) is a free language-learning app, with an optional paid plan, run by Imaginative Prompting. You can reach us at imaginativeprompting@gmail.com about anything on this page.

What we keep on our servers, and why

Your account

  • An account id, the name you choose (shown on anything you share), and when you joined.
  • Your devices: each device you sign in on tells your account a rough label (such as “iPhone, browser”), which packs are on it (their names, languages, whether each is on, and how many sentences), and when it was last seen, so a change can reach all of them. This happens with or without an AI.

How you sign in

  • Passkeys: each passkey’s public key and id, a counter, hints for how your device reaches it, whether it syncs between your devices, and when it was added and last used. Your face, fingerprint or device PIN never leave your device; we never see them.
  • Google: the account id Google gives us for you, and your Google account’s name as a label so you can tell sign-ins apart. We ask Google only for “openid” and “profile”: no email, and nothing else of yours.
  • GitHub: your GitHub user id, and your username as a label. We ask GitHub for no permissions: only your public profile.
  • We use Google’s or GitHub’s answer once, to learn who you are, and don’t keep their tokens.
  • An email, if you add one (Settings → Account → Email): one address, kept only once you’ve typed back a 6-digit code we send to it. It’s for getting back into your account if you lose your passkeys (the address, then a code sent to it, then a new passkey on that device), and for receipts. It never signs you in by itself, is never shown to anyone and never links accounts, and we send it nothing else. We email it when it’s used to get back in, and email the old address when you change it. A code is kept only as a hash, for 15 minutes, and is gone once it’s used, or within the hour after. So that no one can use us to flood an inbox, we also note each code we send, with the address only as a hash, for an hour; deleting your account takes your name off those notes at once.
  • Sessions: when you sign in we set a cookie. We keep only a hash of it, when it started and when it ends, and your browser’s name (its user-agent string), so the sessions in your export can be told apart.

Your progress, your packs and your texts

  • The files your devices write to keep your progress in step, kept with your account: every answer you give (what it was on, whether it was right, and when), your streak and what you finished each day, your place on the path, your word bank, your highlights, where you are in each book, your sound settings, and how you like to study (your choices in Settings → Study and in Sound and speech, except this device’s own voice and speech recognizer). They’re here so that signing in on another device, or on the same one after its browser has cleared what it kept, brings your progress back. We store them as your devices write them and use them for nothing else. They aren’t encrypted from us. They’re in your download, and they’re deleted at once when you delete your account.
  • Your own packs, kept the same way: the packs you make, the ones your AI makes for you, the decks you add and your copies of shared packs, each in a file of its own, with the ones you’ve deleted marked so they stay deleted. They’re here so a pack reaches every device you sign in on, even one you sign in on later. Their recordings aren’t kept here: they stay on the device that has them, and in your own storage if you connect one.
  • Your texts, kept the same way. A text you paste or open from a file, or one your AI adds for you, is kept whole, with the layers your AI wove into it and the cover it drew. These may be your own private writing: we keep them only so they reach every device you sign in on, and use them for nothing else. A book from the open shelf or Explore is kept only as an entry in your library (its title, where it came from and when you added it), and another device fetches it again from there when you open it. Each is in a file of its own (a text too long for one, past about ten novels, stays on your device, and Read says so), and the ones you delete are marked so they stay deleted. They’re in your download, and they’re deleted at once when you delete your account.

If you connect an AI

  • When you connect an AI app (Claude, Codex or another app that speaks MCP), we keep the permission you gave: which app, what it may do and when. Its tokens are stored only as hashes.
  • Personal access tokens you make: their name, the first few characters, and a hash. Never the token itself.
  • Your study record: while an AI is connected, and only then, your device sends a copy of your study after you study, so your AI can coach you: where you are on the path, what each lesson taught, how strong each word and point of grammar is, what you struggle with, and the sentences you missed, with the game and what you put. It’s one copy per language, replaced each time. It goes when you disconnect your last AI, and with no AI connected, none of it leaves your device. Your AI’s provider receives what your AI reads, as below.

What you keep here, and an AI you connect

  • Files and notes you upload, or an AI you connect, such as audio for your lessons.
  • Packs: if you connect an AI, your own packs (what it makes for you, decks you add, your own copies of shared packs), so it sees the ones your devices have and can change them. Without one, only a pack you’ve shared, so a fix you accept reaches your copy. (Every pack of yours is also kept with your account, as above.)
  • Your app’s look: its theme, colors, custom CSS and images, your saved themes, and the last few changes, so a change can be undone. An AI you connect can read and change it if you let it.
  • Practice with an AI you connect: while a practice runs, its turns pass through our server between the app and your AI. Once your device has collected a finished practice, the server deletes it. Your app also keeps a short summary of what you know here, so your AI can choose what to practice. A practice with the plan’s included AI is kept the same way, and goes the same way.
  • What you ask an AI you connect: your requests and their answers wait in your account, and finished ones go after 30 days.
  • Texts and recordings an AI you connect adds: they pass through our server on their way to your devices, and leave once your devices have them, or after 7 days. We keep a list of those changes.
  • A text of yours you ask your AI to weave: pressing Weave it with your AI sends that one text to our server, kept in your account only for your AI to read. It's deleted when your AI has woven it, says it can't, or you cancel (a request no AI takes ends after 14 days, and its copy with it), and a copy no request ever used goes within the hour. The woven layer then passes to your devices as above.

Reminders, if you turn them on

  • For each device, the address its browser gives us for sending notifications, and the keys that lock them.
  • Your reminder settings, your time zone, the language you’re learning, which of the last two weeks’ days you studied or paused, how many things are due, when you last opened the app, and the week’s counts (days, words, chapters). Never what you studied.
  • All of it goes when you turn reminders off on your last device.

Translate

  • When you’re online, what you type also goes to our server, to search Lexicommons’ packs, shared packs and books. It’s used for that search and not kept.
  • Translate with AI on the plan sends what you typed through our server to OpenRouter (below). It isn’t kept.

If you buy the plan

  • Your payment: Stripe handles it. Your card, the email Stripe sends your receipts to, and the record of each payment stay with Stripe; we never see your card. If your account has an email, the first checkout starts with it filled in, and you can change it there. We keep Stripe’s id for you as a customer, and your plan’s state: monthly or yearly, when the period you’ve paid for ends, whether it’s set to cancel, whether it’s paused or has ended and why, and whether you’ve had a trial.
  • Stripe’s notices: Stripe tells our server when a payment or a plan changes. We keep which notices we’ve handled (Stripe’s id for each, its kind and when), with no account on them.
  • A free trial: if your plan came with one, a fingerprint Stripe makes of the card (never the card), kept without your account, so that one card gets one trial.
  • The included AI: for each call, when, what it was for (a practice’s reply, its summary, or Translate with AI), the model, how many tokens went in and out, what it cost and OpenRouter’s id for it; and the month’s total, so your month’s share can be counted. Never what you or the AI wrote.
  • Lexicommons Cloud: what your devices sync, kept on our servers in place of your own storage: your packs and their recordings, your study progress, streak and place on the path, your word bank, highlights, and where you are in each book.

When you use the community

  • What you share (packs, books with their woven layers, corrections, requests, answers to requests, suggestions to a language’s grammar), with your account name and the license it’s shared under. A book’s text keeps its own rights and says where it came from; its layers are yours, under CC BY-SA 4.0.
  • Your likes and follows (and the books you add to your reading library from Explore), the reports you make, and that you accepted the sharing terms.
  • A copyright notice includes what you write in it: who you are, the work, and how to reach you. A moderator sees the work it names, and an admin sees all of it. The person whose item it’s about is told the work it names, but not who sent it or how to reach them.
  • A counter-notice includes why the item should come back, your name and an email. The moderators see why; an admin sees all of it.
  • What moderators do, so it can be checked and undone.

If you report a bug

  • What you write in Settings → Report a bug (or on the sign-in pages’ Report a bug, if you can’t sign in), and a screenshot if you add one. Your device redraws the screenshot before it’s sent, which leaves out anything a photo’s file can carry besides the picture, such as where it was taken.
  • What goes along with it, which the page shows you before you send it: the screen you were on before Settings (or the sign-in page you came from), the app’s version, your browser’s name and version (such as “Safari 26 on iOS”), whether it’s a phone, a tablet or a computer, whether you opened the app from your Home Screen, and the language you’re learning and the one you learn it from.
  • Sent from Settings, it’s linked to your account: it’s in your data’s export, and it’s deleted with your account. Sent from the sign-in pages, it names no one: no account, and we don’t keep your IP address with it.
  • Our admins read reports to put right what went wrong. Nobody else sees them.

Keeping the site safe

  • To stop people guessing passwords or flooding the site, we count recent requests by IP address. The counts last about a minute and aren’t stored with your account.
  • Our host keeps logs of requests and errors for up to 7 days so we can fix problems. A log can hold the address a request asked for (the words of an Explore search are part of it), your IP address and your browser.
  • Error reports: when something breaks in the app or on our server, we keep what broke, in our own database and nowhere else: the error’s message, with anything in it that could be yours taken out on your device before it’s sent (quoted words, links, email addresses, long numbers), which part of our code it was in, which screen of the app, the app’s version, and your browser’s name and version (such as “Safari 26 on iOS”). Never your account, your IP address or a page’s address. The same error is counted together, so a report is a count, not a record of you. When the app on your phone’s Home Screen starts again by itself (a phone does this when it had to close the app while you were using it), the next start tells us that, with only the screen it was on, the app’s version and your browser’s name and version.

What stays on your device

We never see these unless you choose to send them somewhere (reminders and an AI you connect, an AI you connect and the plan use what’s listed above):

  • your practices with an AI you connect, once your device has them;
  • the Anki decks you add: the file is read on your device and never sent to us. The pack made from one is kept with your account, as above, and its sounds never leave this device;
  • your settings, and the connections to your own cloud storage;
  • a key of your own for the in-app AI, if you add one: it stays on this device, sealed, and is never sent to us or synced. Signing out removes it;
  • speech recognition with Whisper, which runs entirely on your device;
  • what you do when you try lessons without an account (lexicommons.com/try): it stays in that browser alone, kept apart from any account, and is cleared the next time you open the try-out or the app. Nothing of it is sent to us.

The app uses your browser’s storage for these. Clearing the site’s data in your browser removes them, so export first if you want a copy.

Other services that see some of your data

  • Cloudflare hosts Lexicommons: its servers run the site and hold the database and files above, and send the emails above, from account@lexicommons.com.
  • Google and GitHub, if you sign in with them, know that you used Lexicommons.
  • If you connect an AI, its provider (such as Anthropic or OpenAI) receives what your AI reads or writes through Lexicommons, under your own agreement with them.
  • Stripe, if you buy the plan, takes the payment and keeps its records, under Stripe’s privacy policy.
  • OpenRouter, for the plan’s included AI: our server sends it what each call needs: a practice’s turns and a summary of what you know in the language, or what you typed into Translate with AI. Every request asks for providers that keep nothing (zero data retention) and don’t train on it. With it goes a tag for your account that’s the same each time and can’t be traced back to you outside Lexicommons: never your name or your account id.
  • The provider of a key of your own (OpenRouter), if you add one for the in-app AI: your device sends it what a practice needs (its turns, and a summary of what you know in the language) straight from here, under your own agreement with them, and asks it to keep nothing. We never see the key. The practice’s turns pass through our practice room as any practice does, and are deleted once your device has them.
  • Your own storage (Google Drive, Dropbox or OneDrive), if you connect it: the app talks to it straight from your device. For Google Drive and OneDrive, our server helps exchange the sign-in code and renew the sign-in, passing the answer straight back to your device, and keeps nothing.
  • Your browser’s speech recognition is what speaking games and Translate’s dictation use unless you download Whisper and choose it: your browser may send the audio to its maker (Google for Chrome, Apple for Safari).
  • Your browser’s notification service, if you turn on reminders (Apple, Google, Mozilla or Microsoft, by your browser): it carries each notification, locked so it can’t read the words.
  • OpenAI: to review something that was reported, a moderator may use OpenAI’s Codex. Only the reported item’s text and what the report says go to it, without your account name or a way to reach you.

How long we keep it

  • Your account and what it holds: until you delete it.
  • A sign-in lasts 90 days at most, then you sign in again.
  • An email you add: until you remove it, or delete your account. A code sent to it: 15 minutes, and gone within the hour after.
  • Our host’s logs: up to 7 days.
  • Error reports: 30 days after that error was last seen; the counts of them by the hour, 14 days.
  • Your progress kept with your account: until you clear it (Settings → Your data) or delete your account.
  • Bug reports: 90 days after you send one, its screenshot too; at once if you delete your account.
  • When you disconnect an AI app, or delete your account, we keep a note that the connection was removed, so it can’t come back: only a scrambled code made from the connection’s and the account’s ids, which names no one, for 90 days.
  • Deleting your account removes it, what it holds and its files from the database at once. Our host’s database backups keep changes for up to 30 days, then they’re gone too.
  • The plan: deleting your account ends it at Stripe first, then its records here go with the account. Stripe keeps its payment records as the law requires; its policy says how long.
  • Lexicommons Cloud: 30 days after the plan ends, so you can move to your own storage, then it’s deleted. Deleting your account deletes it at once.
  • Which of Stripe’s notices we’ve handled, a dispute’s status, and a trial’s card fingerprint, are kept, with no account on them.
  • What you shared with the community stays shared, because its license lets everyone keep using it: your packs, books, corrections and answers are shown as from “a former member”. Your requests and your grammar suggestions go with the account, though what was accepted into a grammar stays, which never named you.
  • Reports you made, copyright notices, counter-notices and what moderators did stay as a record, without your account on them. A notice or a counter-notice keeps the name and contact written in it.

Your choices and rights

  • See and download what our server holds about you, plus what’s on your device: Settings → Your data → Download. Security values (hashes and keys) stay out, and your files, pictures and books are listed rather than copied into it.
  • Change your name, add or remove ways to sign in, and add, change or remove your email: Settings → Account.
  • Disconnect any AI app, or revoke a token: Settings → AI.
  • Cancel the plan: Settings → Plan.
  • Delete your account and everything with it: Settings → Your data.

Depending on where you live (for example in the EU, the UK or California), you have rights to access, correct, delete and take your data with you, and to object to how it’s used. The settings above do all of these; for anything else, write to us. We hold your account’s data to provide the service you signed up for, and use request counts and logs because keeping the site safe is in everyone’s interest. If you’re in the EU or UK, you can also complain to your data protection authority.

Children

Lexicommons isn’t meant for children under 13, and we don’t knowingly let them make an account. Where the law says an older age needs a parent’s permission, the same applies. Buying the plan needs someone 18 or over, or of age where they live: a younger person’s parent or guardian can buy it for them.

Where it’s kept

Cloudflare runs Lexicommons in data centers around the world, and its database may be in the United States. Using Lexicommons means your data may be handled outside your country.
